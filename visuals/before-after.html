What are we actually improving?

Same clinic, same 200 patients, same 21,000 notes. The only thing that changes is whether the notes are reachable.

The goal: today a clinic worker can filter the structured columns, but the story of every visit is locked in free-text notes that only match on exact letters. We make those notes searchable by meaning (vector search), so people can ask plain-English questions across patients and get a summarized, cited answer instead of a pile of rows.

1 · Find by meaning

“Struggling to breathe” finds notes that say dyspnea on exertion or winded at the mailbox. No shared words needed.

2 · Summarize across patients

“What’s the pattern?” is answered in one paragraph, not 40 notes opened one at a time.

3 · Show the source

Every claim links back to the note it came from, so a clinician can verify it.

What we are not claiming. Structured questions (counts, dates, lab values) were never the problem, and SQL still answers them exactly. Vector search is the missing half, not a replacement. It can also be fooled (negations, near-misses), which is why we cover re-ranking, evals, and citations later in the course.

30-second talk track for class

“Our clinic’s database has two halves. The structured half (diagnoses, meds, labs) is easy: SQL answers it in milliseconds. The story half (21,000 visit notes) is where the doctor writes why the patient came in and what they saw.”

“Search that half the old way and you only match letters. Ask for ‘short of breath’ and get nothing, because the note says ‘dyspnea.’ Same fact, zero shared words.”

“So we turn every note into a vector, a point in meaning-space, and search by meaning. Then an LLM reads the best matches and writes the answer, with sources. That’s the entire project: make the other half of the data reachable.”

The patients, notes, and numbers here are hand-authored to make the idea obvious. They are illustrations, not live model output (same as the other visuals). Every patient is synthetic.
